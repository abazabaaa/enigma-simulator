# Trace the Wire, Then Prove It

No existing tool combines a 3D Enigma, a per-stage signal trace, historically complete mechanics and mastery gating, so the product described in this brief is an unoccupied niche rather than a crowded one. Dozens of simulators compute Enigma correctly, but only a handful expose the mechanism (Daniel Palloks' arrow-overlaid Universal Enigma, the trace-panel tools, reimunyancat's unlicensed 3D "X-ray" build), the two photoreal 3D experiences (Martin Gillow's CC BY 4.0 Virtual Enigma and Virtual Bombe) show procedure but hide the circuit, and the best-received explainer of all is a non-interactive Blender video that has drawn **14 million views** by following one wire at a time ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ybkkiGtJmkM)). On the codebreaking side, every bombe simulator defers the *why* of loop-based rejection to Graham Ellsbury's 1998 prose, and no browser tool anywhere implements Rejewski's cycle-structure attack interactively. The learning-science evidence that should shape the design is unusually consistent: representational animation (d ≈ 0.40), learner-paced segments (d ≈ 0.70), signalling (d ≈ 0.46), prediction-before-reveal, mastery over several randomised items (d ≈ 0.50 on taught objectives) and stories about real people (g ≈ 0.16 in science instruction, with fiction underperforming) all point to a step-driven, one-component-per-scene course where the learner bets on the outcome before every animation and passes gates on two of three fresh instances. Because the target learner codes with AI agents, code exercises cannot open gates on their own; each must be paired with a typed prediction or a "set the machine" task that a pasted solution cannot answer. The recommendation below specifies a four-act, twelve-chapter course on React 19.3 + React Three Fiber 9.8 + three 0.186, a procedurally modelled machine, a ~150-line TypeScript engine validated against eight published vectors and three independent oracles, an XState lesson machine, and a 2D wire-state bombe view that fills the field's most conspicuous gap.

## Dozens of tools compute Enigma, a handful expose the mechanism, none gate understanding

The simulator field splits cleanly into operator-experience tools and mechanism explainers, and the split is the first design lesson. Dirk Rijmenants' EnigmaSim v7.0.6 lets users "actually lift out and insert different rotors, adjust their ring setting and set up the plugboard" with photorealistic imagery and wartime sample messages, but its page describes no signal-path display and it last updated in December 2022 ([Cipher Machines and Cryptology](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmasim.htm)). Franklin Heath's Android app, rated **4.7 stars across 2,200 reviews with 100k+ installs** and still updated in June 2026, is "designed to give the experience of using a real Enigma machine, as far as possible within the limitations of a flat screen", again with rotors, lamps and paper tape but no path view ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=uk.co.franklinheath.enigmasim&hl=en_GB)). Martin Gillow's Virtual Enigma, a three.js/Blender scene covering Enigma I and M4 under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence, lets users swap rotors, plug the stecker and set Ringstellung, and even operate a Morse transceiver, but neither its site nor its technical page describes a live trace ([Virtual Enigma](https://enigma.virtualcolossus.co.uk/); [technical page](https://enigma.virtualcolossus.co.uk/technical.html)). These tools teach *procedure* well and earn their ratings for fidelity, while hiding the circuit the brief wants to make concrete.

The mechanism explainers are the other half. Palloks' Universal Enigma v2.6.2 covers 17 historical models and offers two monitoring views, a "wiring display" of every component's substitution and a "signal path view" that "displays green/red arrows tracing signal flow through the machine with rotor positions and turnover points marked"; the author states it incorporates double-stepping and was validated against museum machines, and it may be used, distributed and modified freely provided authorship is acknowledged, modifications are indicated and open status is preserved ([Palloks](https://palloks.2ix.de/enigma/index_en.html)). Palloks' turnover markers on the path view are the only instance found where stepping context and path share one frame, but the tool's own community notes "there are a lot of variables on the screen, not all of which are useful for every Enigma machine" ([Arduino Enigma blog](http://arduinoenigma.blogspot.com/2025/02/daniel-palloks-universal-enigma.html)). CrypTool-Online's "Enigma (step-by-step)" lets users "inspect the encryption and decryption of the Enigma cipher machine step by step" and warns when a configuration "is impossible with a physical (standard) enigma", under an Apache-2.0 plugin repository ([legacy CrypTool](https://legacy.cryptool.org/en/cto/enigma-step-by-step); [cto LICENSE](https://github.com/cryptool-org/cto/blob/master/LICENSE)). The newer SEO-style tools do the same in tabular form: mtool.io shows per keystroke that "wheels step first, then current flows through the plugboard, rotors and reflector, and returns to the lamp" ([mtool.io](https://mtool.io/en/enigma-machine)), and DecodeCipher pairs a trace panel with a "Show internal wiring" toggle that reveals the effective alphabet with ring settings applied ([DecodeCipher](https://decodecipher.net/enigma-machine.html)). Tabular traces are the most accurate and cheapest visualisation to build, and every one of them is visually abstract.

Three browser 3D builds exist, and each falls short in a different way. reimunyancat's project ships "an X-ray mode" with a "transparent casing [that] reveals internal wiring with current path tracing", drag-and-drop plugboard cables and six 1941 Wehrmacht intercepts, on a C++17 engine compiled to WebAssembly under Svelte and three.js, but it models Enigma I only, has no licence file and zero stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/reimunyancat/enigma)). enigmasimulator.com promised "an innovative 3D interactive experience" on Hacker News in June 2025 but its scraped body is an author biography, so its features are unverified ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44224977)). Virtual Enigma is licensed and polished but drew the sharpest usability critique in the corpus: "when I see a 3D visual interface I expect an intuitive navigation and interaction, that doesn't need a manual or tutorial", to which the developer replied by promising "a simpler entry point (maybe startup basic keys panel)" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/virtual-enigma-simulation/27494)). Tom MacWright's d3 "through-the-barrel" notebook animated the path but a Hacker News reader listed missing "ring settings, ground/indicator settings, double-step anomaly, and decryption", took "15 seconds just to see one letter get encrypted", and concluded it was "half-finished but published" ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20843958)). The critiques never mention polish; they name missing mechanics, unclear affordances and speed.

The video explainers show what the interactive tools lack. Jared Owen's "How did the Enigma Machine work?" (uploaded 2021-12-11, 19:25, **14,020,857 views**, rendered in Blender 3.0 with internals rebuilt by hand after the creator consulted a museum owner who took apart a real machine) builds the circuit up from "a battery, some wires, and ... a light bulb", asks "what if we scramble these wires?", follows a single wire through a rotor cutaway ("follow the wire through and it comes out the pin for the number four ... an A was changed to a D"), counts that "the letter was changed seven times", cuts away a plugboard socket to reveal the shorting bar, and animates the pawl dropping into the notch so "the second rotor is allowed to spin, but just once" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ybkkiGtJmkM)). Kottke called it "one of the best explanations I've ever seen of anything on YouTube" ([Kottke](https://kottke.org/26/08/how-does-an-enigma-machine-work)) and Crypto Museum embeds it on its own working-principle page ([Crypto Museum](https://www.cryptomuseum.com/crypto/enigma/working.htm)). Veritasium's 47-minute engineering deep-dive, published 2026-09-21, reached **6.7 million views in five days** ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JsBZOcqZerk)), and Numberphile's 2013 demonstration with a real machine sits at 6.7 million ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G2_Q9FoD-oQ)). Educators reach the same conclusion from paper: Cornell's CS3110 assignment tells students that "a 3D model is worth a million words: the Pringles-can model ... makes these examples considerably easier to follow" while dropping ring settings for simplicity ([Cornell CS3110](https://cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs3110/2020sp/a1)), and Franklin Heath's paper Enigma flags the double step as behaviour that "is often misunderstood" and insists learners step "before you read off the letter (even the first one!)" ([Franklin Heath wiki](http://wiki.franklinheath.co.uk/index.php/Enigma/Paper_Enigma)).

Read together, the catalog says that a tool delivering a 3D machine *and* an accurate per-stage trace *and* the mechanics educators usually drop (rings, double step, plugboard traversed twice, reflector as 13 pairs) would exceed every existing tool, and that it should borrow four devices the best explainers share: reduce to one wire and follow it, count the transformations, cut away the opaque parts, and show stepping as pawl-meets-notch rather than as a rule.

## Follow one wire, bet before the reveal, step rather than scroll: the methods with evidence

The strongest exemplars of interactive explanation converge on a scene grammar that maps directly onto a 3D Enigma. Bartosz Ciechanowski's "Mechanical Watch" opens with a manipulable model ("you can drag the device around to change your viewing angle, and you can use the slider to peek at what's going on inside"), builds the mechanism one subsystem at a time with obscuring parts temporarily removed, colour-codes every named part, and lets the reader "scrub back and forth in time" ([Ciechanowski](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/)). Nicky Case's rules add the pedagogical scaffolding Ciechanowski omits: teach each mechanic "in isolation first before combining them", "let the explorer create their own data points", withhold the explanation when discovery motivates, and end with a capstone that requires "knowledge the explorer's learnt throughout the explanation" ([Case, 2014](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations/)). His later patterns are the gates themselves: "Puzzle It Out" merges teaching and assessment so players prove understanding before progressing, "Place Your Bets!" has the reader predict before the reveal, and "Sandbox Mode" is warned to risk cognitive overload unless simulation elements are introduced gradually ([Case, 2018](https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/)). Bret Victor's critique is the counterweight that keeps the 3D machine from becoming a toy: sandboxes where users "figure it out for yourself" are "not explanations", and an environment for code must let the reader "follow the flow" and "see the state" because instant output alone "misses the point" ([Victor, Explorable Explanations](http://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/); [Victor, Learnable Programming](http://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/)).

The learning-science literature puts numbers on these intuitions and tells the team where to spend effort. Höffler and Leutner's meta-analysis of 76 comparisons found animation beats static pictures overall at **d = 0.37**, but only representational animation carries the effect (d = 0.40) while decorative motion does not, and highly realistic animation reaches d = 0.76 ([Höffler & Leutner 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475207001077)); the 3D scene must therefore show the causal path of current, not a spinning machine. Mayer's multimedia principles, as summarised from his lab experiments, give spatial contiguity d ≈ 0.79 (labels on the parts, not in a side panel), temporal contiguity d ≈ 1.30, segmenting into learner-paced chunks d ≈ 0.70, signalling d ≈ 0.46 (dim everything but the live wire), coherence d ≈ 0.70 (exclude extraneous material) and pre-training d ≈ 0.46 (teach component names before any calculation) ([LITFL summary of CTML](https://litfl.com/cognitive-theory-of-multimedia-learning/)). Prediction before reveal has a mechanism as well as an effect: Brod's review finds that "predicting boosts surprise about unexpected answers, which leads to enhanced attention to the correct answer and strengthens its encoding", and only generating a prediction produced the pupil-dilation response associated with later retrieval ([Brod 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8642250/)). Every 3D demonstration in the course should therefore ask "which lamp lights?" or "does the middle rotor step?" before it animates.

Active and constructive engagement dominate passive viewing at scale. Freeman's meta-analysis of 225 STEM studies found active learning raised performance by **0.47 standard deviations** and that students in traditional lectures were 1.95 times as likely to fail ([Freeman et al. 2014](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.1319030111)), and the ICAP framework orders engagement Interactive > Constructive > Active > Passive, with constructive activities that generate outputs beyond the given material outperforming mere manipulation ([Chi & Wylie 2014](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823)). Retrieval practice beats restudy only after a delay: Roediger and Karpicke showed recall tests outperformed restudying at two days and one week even though restudy won at five minutes ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)), and Quantum Country's embedded spaced prompts let most readers answer nearly all 112 questions after weeks, at a cost of a 35 to 50 per cent reading-time overhead and only 29 per cent of finishers continuing to the one-month review ([Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zt1TyUANyt84UkQVBJjWEGZ3JUd2HP92r65)). A gate at the end of a scene measures short-term recall; a two- or three-item re-check at the start of the next act is what converts it into retention, and it must stay short.

Mastery gating itself has solid but bounded support. Kulik, Kulik and Bangert-Drowns' review of 108 controlled evaluations found positive effects on exam performance, larger for weaker students (d = 0.61 versus 0.40), but **d = 0.50 on experimenter-made tests against only d = 0.08 on standardised tests** ([Kulik et al. 1990](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060002265); [Nintil summary](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)). Gates should therefore measure the objectives actually taught, where the evidence is strongest, and include at least one transfer instance per act (a different rotor order, a toy alphabet) where it is weakest. The worked-example effect (g = 0.48 across 55 studies) and the expertise-reversal effect (worked examples lose value as knowledge grows, per a 2025 meta-analysis of 176 effect sizes) together argue for faded worked examples in the early machine chapters and problem-first gates once the learner reaches the mathematics ([Educational Psychology Review 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1); [expertise reversal meta-analysis 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475225000660)).

Narrative earns its place but not the whole page. Mar and colleagues' meta-analysis of 33,078 participants found stories better remembered and comprehended than expository text at **g = 0.55**, while warning the result "should not be interpreted as a suggestion to force all information into a narrative form" ([Mar et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8219577/)); a 2024 science-education meta-analysis found a modest g = 0.16 and that "learning from personal or scientist-centered stories was more favorable than learning from fictional stories" ([narrative vs expository meta-analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384420142_Impact_of_Narrative_versus_Expository_Instruction_in_Science_Education_on_Recall_Understanding_and_Transfer_A_Meta-Analysis)). Rejewski, Różycki, Zygalski, Turing and Welchman are the characters; invented ones would underperform. Bletchley Park's own school workshop uses exactly this frame, set "in 1941 during the Battle of the Atlantic, where students take on the role of codebreakers" ([Bletchley Park KS3](https://www.bletchleypark.org.uk/event/key-stage-3/)), and the repository's existing notebook already opens with "midnight at Bletchley" and a new day key while admitting that Rejewski's method predates Bletchley ([BreakingEnigma.ipynb](/home/user/enigma-simulator/BreakingEnigma.ipynb)). The one caution is metacomprehension: informative narratives can make learners feel they understood more than they did, so every act must end on a non-narrative gate.

The final structural choice is scroll versus click. Distill's review of interactive articles reports that McKenna and colleagues found no engagement difference between scrollytelling and stepper layouts on desktop while Zhi and colleagues found better comprehension with steppers, and it notes that mobile is understudied and that "not everything needs to be interactive" ([Hohman et al., Distill 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)). Gates need discrete, addressable states, and the only comparative evidence favours steppers for comprehension, so click-driven progression should be the default with scroll reserved for cinematic camera moves inside a scene.

## Bombe tools teach procedure, not logic, and Rejewski's mathematics has no interactive explainer

Four browser-runnable Turing–Welchman bombe simulators exist, and their strengths are the inverse of the command-line tools'. Ekhall and Hallenberg's Lysator simulator, "first created for the Alan Turing Year 2012" and ported from Flash to HTML5 in 2020, models the front, sides and back of the bombe plus a checking machine and publishes twenty crib-bearing challenge messages with a leaderboard of first solvers, but it offers no source or licence, describes its HTML5 port as beta, and its live index returned HTTP 500 on the research date ([Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20250101102109/https://www.lysator.liu.se/~koma/turingbombe/)). George Lasry called its challenges "a highly enjoyable process" ([Cipherbrain](https://scienceblogs.de/klausis-krypto-kolumne/2019/02/06/a-turing-bombe-simulator-and-an-unsolved-enigma-challenge/)). Gillow's Virtual Bombe (2023, CC BY 4.0) is the most complete end-to-end teaching path: a menu generator in which "two letter S in the text are shown in red, this is because we know that Enigma can not encipher a letter as itself", drum placement, rear-panel wiring, a stop recorded as "SNY:D", a checking machine with "a yellow dummy for 3-rotor Enigmas", and hand-off to Virtual Enigma ([Virtual Bombe simulation](https://bombe.virtualcolossus.co.uk/simulation.html)). Yet for the logic its technical page says "I recommend a visit to www.ellsbury.com, which has a very good detailed description" ([Virtual Bombe technical](https://bombe.virtualcolossus.co.uk/technical.html)). owenautosport's MIT-licensed 3D bombe (three.js r128, 2026) adds the only step-gated tour in the field, where "each step dims the page, lights up the control you need to press next, and waits until you've pressed it — or does it for you with 'Do it for me'", runs "all 60 wheel orders in seconds, or step at 1940 speed", and includes Welchman's diagonal board and a test register, but the repository has five commits ([GitHub](https://github.com/owenautosport/enigma-bombe)). 101computing's classroom simulator uses one drum bank and "generates less false stops than a real Turing-Welchman Bombe" because it silently runs the checking-machine test ([101computing](https://www.101computing.net/turing-welchman-bombe/)). GCHQ's CyberChef Bombe (Apache-2.0) is the safest logic core, auto-building the menu from a crib and running the checking machine by default so that a three-rotor run "completes in about a fifth of a second", but its designers removed manual menu construction because it would "add extra barriers to users experimenting", the exact opposite of a teaching gate ([CyberChef wiki](https://github.com/gchq/CyberChef/wiki/Enigma,-the-Bombe,-and-Typex); [Bombe.mjs](https://github.com/gchq/CyberChef/blob/master/src/core/operations/Bombe.mjs)).

What none of them shows is the state of the 26 wires as a hypothesis propagates, and that is where every good explainer says the understanding lives. Ellsbury slides the crib WETTERVORHERSAGEBISKAYA along the ciphertext until no letter coincides, draws the menu with "square boxes represent scramblers and the numbers in the boxes represent the positions", and states the loop criterion that makes the bombe intelligible: "a steckering hypothesis is consistent if and only if, having returned via some chain of implications to its origin, it implies itself" ([Ellsbury ch. 1](http://www.ellsbury.com/bombe1.htm)). Because "the circuitry of the bombe does not readily lend itself to being diagrammed", he reduces the alphabet to eight letters, wires the complete simplified bombe, and shows that at the correct position the test register ends with either 25 live wires (false hypothesis) or 1 live wire (true hypothesis), "the logical complement of each other" ([Ellsbury ch. 3](http://www.ellsbury.com/bombe3.htm); [Ellsbury ch. 4](http://www.ellsbury.com/bombe4.htm)). Tony Sale gives the same test in a sentence, "if either only one or 25 of the lamps are lit. If all 26 lamps light then this position can be rejected" ([Tony Sale](https://www.codesandciphers.org.uk/virtualbp/tbombe/tbombe.htm)), and Jennifer Wilcox's NSA appendix supplies the practical menu rule, "the Bombe required two such closures ... It also required a total of thirteen or fourteen links" ([Wilcox appendix](https://websites.nku.edu/~christensen/Bombe.pdf)). Turing's own stop analysis quantifies why loops matter: an 8-letter crib with three closures yields about **2.2 expected stops per wheel order, one closure yields 1,500, and none yields 40,000**, which is why standard practice used menus estimated at no more than four stops per wheel order and why longer cribs risked a mid-crib middle-rotor turnover ([Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe); [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)). A documented 12-letter menu, ciphertext WSNPNLKLSTCS over crib ATTACKATDAWN with loops ATLK, TNS and TAWCN, has no crashes and three closures, and was re-verified for this research ([Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe)). ProtoBombe's `enableDiagonalBoard` switch, which lets a user "see the difference it makes", is a small idea worth copying into a gate ([ProtoBombe](https://github.com/ctigeek/ProtoBombe)); the recurring misconception it corrects is that the diagonal board was part of Turing's original design ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=18030711)).

The Polish side is thinner still. Bill Casselman, writing for the AMS in 2009, judged that "Nothing that I have found on the Internet explains the mathematics well", and supplied the two devices that make Rejewski's theorem concrete: a drastically simplified one-rotor machine and the trick of writing one cycle backwards beneath its equal-length partner so that only 13 alignments remain ([Casselman, AMS](https://www.ams.org/publicoutreach/feature-column/fcarc-enigma)). James Grime's handout adds the physical picture of "two Enigmas wired together so J becomes Q" and the punchline that "you could ignore the plugboard! ... The two plugboards in the middle aren't necessary because they cancel each other out" ([Grime](https://www.singingbanana.com/enigmaproject/maths.pdf)). The only code is Python: the NSA's MIT-licensed `rejewski.py` builds AD, BE and CF dictionaries from doubled indicators, extracts chains, and produces an index string such as `"AD:5431 BE:621 CF:8341"` for catalogue lookup ([rejewski.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NationalSecurityAgency/enigma-simulator/master/rejewski.py); [NSA repo](https://github.com/NationalSecurityAgency/enigma-simulator)), and Jean-François Bouchaudy's cyclometer CLI prints signatures like `13-13 TQKUHBYWZOMSI:XVGFNARCEDLPJ IV,I,II ZZZ AAA` alongside a fully worked catalogue lookup that narrows to a single Grundstellung and then deduces steckers from cycle letters ([jfbouch cyclometer](http://www.jfbouch.fr/crypto/enigma/tools/cyclo.html); [jfbouch catalogue](http://www.jfbouch.fr/crypto/enigma/methods/catalog.html)). Tony Sale's Zygalski-sheet pages survive as 1990s client-side JavaScript that "recreates the overlaid portion of each sheet at run time" ([Tony Sale, Zygalski](https://codesandciphers.org.uk/anoraks/zygalsht.htm)), and Casselman's second column gives the numbers to animate: about 40 per cent of rotor configurations show a female, so "if we find 12 females, we can reduce the probable number to about 1 or 2" ([Casselman, Zygalski sheets](https://www.ams.org/publicoutreach/feature-column/fc-2013-12)).

The ground truth for these chapters is precise enough to compute live. Rejewski's worked example of 65 indicators yields **AD = (a)(bc)(dvpfkxgzyo)(eijmunqlht)(rw)(s)** with cycle lengths 10 10 2 2 1 1, BE with 9 9 3 3 1 1, and CF with 13 13; the conjugation theorem then means "the disjoint cycle structure of AD is the same as it would be if there were no plugboard; the effect of the plugboard has been nullified!", and the plugboard-free search space collapses to 6 × 17,576 = 105,456 rotor states ([Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)). Wikipedia puts the number of possible characteristics at "roughly 1 million" against those 105,456 starting positions ([Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)); since each product has cycles in equal-length pairs, its type is a doubled partition of 13, of which there are 101, giving 101³ = 1,030,301 signature buckets, a figure worth animating as most buckets empty or tiny. Rejewski himself reported that once the catalogues were ready "obtaining a daily key was usually a matter of ten to twenty minutes" ([Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)). Two theorems carry the whole act and both animate on six letters: the product of two fixed-point-free involutions has cycles in equal-length pairs (X = (ab)(cd)(ef), Y = (bc)(de)(fa) gives XY = (ace)(bfd), two 3-cycles running opposite ways), and "two permutations are conjugate if and only if they have the same cycle structure", which cryptologist A. Deavours called "the theorem that won World War II" ([Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski); [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)).

Games confirm the danger of mechanics without scaffolding. Cypher's Enigma chapter drew the complaint that "the game has a very short explanation of how the Enigma works, which isn't really enough for players with zero previous knowledge", that it "leans almost entirely towards being an educational tool ... and then fails at educating sufficiently", and that solving "feels like calculating a crap ton of letters to find the answer rather than solving a puzzle" ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1823899009); [Glitchwave](https://glitchwave.com/game/cypher/)). The 2026 jam game "Bletchley" shows the narrative device that does work, "narrative fragments appear with no UI or score" between levels following Turing from 1939 to 1954, with rotor-sweeping crib hints as the mechanic ([dev.to](https://dev.to/crubio/bletchley-a-codebreaker-game-about-winning-the-war-and-losing-the-man-48jd)). None of the games gates progress on understanding a loop or a cycle.

## An agent-assisted programmer can outsource code, so every code gate needs a paired prediction

Implementing the small functions that *are* the mechanism is the highest-value activity for this audience and the easiest to fake. ICAP places constructive work, producing outputs beyond what was presented, above active manipulation ([Chi & Wylie 2014](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823)); Zed Shaw's method rests on the same generation effect, insisting learners type code "precisely (no copy-and-paste!)" because "if you copy and paste, you might as well just not even do them", in a sequence that has students "do exercises, then explain them, then apply them" ([PyCon 2011 interview](https://pycon.blogspot.com/2011/02/title-pycon-2011-interview-with-zed.html)). A learner who codes with agents will paste a working `enigma_keypress` in seconds, and a green test suite then measures the agent, not the person. The intelligent-tutoring literature already names this: "gaming the system" means "exploiting system properties rather than engaging in meaningful learning" through "systematic guessing or abusing hints", and it is consistently associated with lower learning gains ([Baker et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221413987_Adapting_to_When_Students_Game_an_Intelligent_Tutoring_System)). Koedinger and Aleven's "assistance dilemma" gives the remedy shape: initially withhold solution information, then "interactively add information, only as needed, through yes/no feedback, explanatory hints, and dynamic problem selection" ([Koedinger & Aleven 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-007-9049-0)).

The gate design that follows is a pairing rule. A code exercise opens a gate only together with a prediction typed before the run ("what will `cycle_lengths(AD)` return for this instance?"), an explanation selection ("why does toggling a stecker leave these lengths unchanged?"), or a follow-up "set the machine" item derived from the code's output; the code then serves the learner as a tool, as the repository notebook intends, while the gate measures understanding. Parsons problems, in which learners order given code or procedure blocks, achieved "the same learning gains as writing or fixing code, in less time" ([Ericson et al. 2017](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3141880.3141895)) and are the cheap check for the *sequence* of the signal path or of Rejewski's attack. Execute Program's format of hundreds of tiny examples where the learner supplies the expected result, with spaced reviews that "intelligently unlock lessons only when learners are ready", is the retrieval-practice form of a coding check ([Execute Program](https://www.executeprogram.com/spaced-repetition)); a reviewer's note that "related reviews all come on the same day, which makes them artificially easy" is the trap to avoid ([mike.place](https://mike.place/2020/executeprogram/)). Khan Academy's ladder, Familiar at some correct answers, Proficient at more, Mastered only when the skill is also answered correctly on a unit test, is the model for never gating on a single item ([Khan Academy](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853--How-do-Khan-Academy-s-Mastery-levels-work)). Duolingo's hearts, where mistakes cost lives that regenerate over hours and streaks get gamed by "the bare minimum lesson", are the punitive economy to keep out of the assessment layer ([Medium](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/duolingo-the-product-that-gamified-learning-and-made-it-addictive-6733f2b56307)); the ticking clock belongs to the story layer.

The runtime question is settled by bytes. CodeMirror 6's view and state packages weigh **77 kB and 16 kB gzipped** ([bundlephobia](https://bundlephobia.com/api/size?package=@codemirror/view@6.43.13)), Replit cut total JavaScript downloads by over 40 per cent moving from Monaco to CodeMirror ([Replit](https://blog.replit.com/codemirror)), and a same-origin Web Worker with a kill timeout is an adequate sandbox for a static site with no secrets, upgradable to a QuickJS-WASM sandbox (quickjs-emscripten 0.32.0, MIT) if deterministic limits and zero DOM access are wanted ([npm registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/quickjs-emscripten)). Pyodide "brings the CPython interpreter to the web via WebAssembly" and has been used to grade code "against a test suite in a web worker" with "instant feedback and no need for server resources" ([Pyodide blog issue](https://github.com/pyodide/pyodide-blog/issues/60)), but older docs quote a 6.4 MB first load and 4–5 s initialisation ([Pyodide roadmap](https://pyodide.org/en/0.18.0/project/roadmap.html)) and the full distribution exceeds 200 MB ([Pyodide deploy docs](https://pyodide.org/en/stable/usage/downloading-and-deploying.html)); it is worth it only if Python is pedagogically required, and then only lazy-loaded in the exercise chapters. Sandpack's bundler must be hosted on a separate origin for cookie isolation ([Sandpack docs](https://sandpack.codesandbox.io/docs/guides/hosting-the-bundler)), which is overkill for ten-line cipher functions. Whichever runner is chosen, Victor's "see the state" argues that the 3D machine should be the visualiser for the learner's own code: when their `enigma_keypress` runs, animate the path it produced and diff it against the reference path, so a failing test rolls the learner back to the exact component that is wrong rather than to a red X.

## Design recommendation: a four-act, twelve-chapter gated course on a verified engine

### UX rules in priority order

The interface is a stepper, not a scroll. Each chapter is a sequence of discrete scenes advanced by a Next control that is enabled only from a "passed" state; scroll is used only for cinematic camera moves within a scene and always has a button equivalent for keyboard and reduced-motion users, following the comprehension evidence for steppers ([Distill 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)). Each scene isolates one component with everything else dimmed and the live wire glowing, with names and equation symbols rendered on the 3D parts themselves rather than in a side panel, so signalling and spatial contiguity work together ([LITFL summary of CTML](https://litfl.com/cognitive-theory-of-multimedia-learning/)); the colour of rotor I in the scene is the colour of the symbol R₁ in every equation. The signal path is shown two ways at once and in sync: a glowing tube through the 3D machine and a per-stage letter trace (plugboard, R, M, L, UKW, L, M, R, plugboard, lamp) in the style of DecodeCipher and CrypTool, with a speed slider and a scrub control so that no learner waits fifteen seconds per letter ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=20843958)). Every animation is preceded by a bet, "which lamp lights?", "does the middle rotor move?", per Brod's generation mechanism ([Brod 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8642250/)). The rotor is drawn as two separately rotatable layers, an alphabet ring carrying the letters and the notch and a wiring core, because "the rotor turnover point remains at the same letter, because the notch is located at the alphabet ring" ([Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)), and the app shows positions as letters and rings as 01–26 numbers to keep the two apart. The reflector is drawn as 13 wire pairs, the plugboard as cables the path visibly crosses twice, and stepping as a pawl meeting a notch ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ybkkiGtJmkM)). The DOM is the source of truth for operation, a hidden or visible group of 26 real `<button>` keys, spinbuttons for rotors and a pair list for plugs, with an `aria-live` region announcing "Q lights E; rotors now A E W", so that "all functionality of the content is operable through a keyboard interface" ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html)), and the 3D canvas mirrors that store. `prefers-reduced-motion` swaps animated paths for fully drawn ones and disables camera fly-throughs rather than removing all motion ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). The first screen is a guided entry with the whole machine manipulable and a single "type a word" affordance, not a free 3D scene, answering the Virtual Enigma critique ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/virtual-enigma-simulation/27494)). Story beats are short interstitials between mechanism scenes, about real people, and never overlaid on a calculation; the ticking clock motivates at act boundaries and never times an assessment. Progressive disclosure follows the Franklin Heath order, rotors and reflector first, then stepping, then rings, then plugboard ([Franklin Heath wiki](http://wiki.franklinheath.co.uk/index.php/Enigma/Paper_Enigma)). Shareable URL state and a paper-tape record with copy/paste round-trip demonstrate reciprocity and support classroom exchange, as reimunyancat and Franklin Heath do ([GitHub](https://github.com/reimunyancat/enigma); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=uk.co.franklinheath.enigmasim&hl=en_GB)).

Gate rules apply everywhere. A gate passes on two of three fresh randomised instances, mirroring the 80–90 per cent mastery criteria of Bloom's studies ([Nintil](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)); every retry draws a new rotor order, position or indicator set so elimination cannot pass it. Assistance is withheld then added: attempt one has no hint, attempt two highlights the relevant component in the 3D scene, attempt three shows a worked example on a *different* instance, attempt four reveals the solution and requires one more fresh instance ([Koedinger & Aleven 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-007-9049-0)). Failure triggers a gentle rollback that animates the learner's own wrong answer through the machine and shows where it diverges. Sub-two-second answers and rapid hint chains switch the next item to a "set the machine" task that cannot be brute-forced ([Baker et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221413987_Adapting_to_When_Students_Game_an_Intelligent_Tutoring_System)). Each act opens with two or three retrieval items from the previous act. There are no hearts, lives or timers on assessment. Code gates require the learner to type functions of at most about ten lines into a provided harness and are always paired with a prediction or explanation item.

The Enigma itself should be modelled procedurally rather than imported. The verified per-keypress order is: step the rotors, then plugboard, ETW, right → middle → left (→ Greek) forward, reflector, (Greek →) left → middle → right backward, ETW, plugboard, lamp; "depressing a key will first step the rotors and then send the current through the rotors and the light bulb" ([Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)). The verified stepping rule is `stepMiddle = right.atTurnover() || middle.atTurnover(); stepLeft = middle.atTurnover(); right.step(); if (stepMiddle) middle.step(); if (stepLeft) left.step();` with the turnover test on the window letter, because the notch is fixed to the ring. The verified ring formula, with position p and ring r zero-based, is offset o = (p − r) mod 26, forward out = (W[(in + o) mod 26] − o) mod 26, backward the same with W⁻¹. Property tests complete the suite: encryption is an involution with no fixed points over thousands of consecutive positions ([Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)); the three-rotor period is 16,900, not 17,576, because of double stepping ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine)); ring and position shifts are equivalent except through carry timing; and the plugboard-pair count table (10 plugs = 150,738,274,937,250, maximum at 11 plugs) reproduces ([Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)). Run the engine in CI against three independent oracles: `@ondoher/enigma` 1.0.14 (MIT, npm, explicitly "provided as a working reference that can be used to validate the simulator being built", with `listen()` hooks on every stage that can assert the intermediate contacts shown in the 3D trace) ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@ondoher/enigma); [GitHub](https://github.com/Ondoher/enigma)), cryptii's Enigma encoder (MIT, wirings cited to Crypto Museum) ([Enigma.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cryptii/cryptii/main/src/Encoder/Enigma.js)), and py-enigma 1.0.2 (MIT) via a one-off script that commits a JSON vector file covering rotors IV/V, extreme rings and 1,000-letter texts ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/py-enigma/)).

### Known pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why it bites | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong double-step sequence | The brief's own "ADU→AEV→BFW" is off by one; the middle rotor carries only when the right rotor moves V→W ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_rotor_details)) | Vectors 3–5; the Enigma.jl author "struggled" with exactly this ([opensourc.es](https://opensourc.es/blog/enigma-and-bombe)) |
| Mixed turnover conventions | Sale's after-carry letters vs Crypto Museum's before-carry letters ([Tony Sale](https://www.codesandciphers.org.uk/enigma/rotorspec.htm)) | Store Q E V J Z and Z+M only |
| Ring vs position confusion | Rings shift wiring and notch relative to letters but not the turnover letter ([Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)) | Two-layer rotor; letters for positions, numbers for rings; vector 2 |
| Encoding before stepping | The first keypress already uses the advanced position ([Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)) | "Step first" shown explicitly as mtool does |
| Reusing the repo's Python engine | `machine.py` steps the *left* rotor and sends the signal L→M→R, `components.py` has no ring settings, no middle-rotor double step, caps plugs at six and lacks rotor IV ([machine.py](/home/user/enigma-simulator/machine.py)) | Write the TypeScript engine fresh; ship the corrected model to any Pyodide oracle |
| Over-generalising "no letter to itself" | In AD/BE/CF products a fixed point is legitimate; the notebook flags this ([BreakingEnigma.ipynb](/home/user/enigma-simulator/BreakingEnigma.ipynb)) | MC probe with the misconception as distractor in II.5 |

### Build order

Priority zero is the engine and the gate framework, because everything else depends on both being right: the ~150-line TypeScript engine with the 14 vectors and property tests in Vitest, the three-oracle CI job, the XState lesson machine with randomised-instance generation, the 2-of-3 pass rule and the four-step assistance ladder, and the DOM-first machine controls with the `aria-live` announcer. Priority one is Act I with the procedural 3D machine, the synced tube-and-table trace, the two-layer rotor, the 6-letter toy variant and the four machine chapters; this alone exceeds every existing simulator on the ranked pattern list and is shippable as a standalone explainer. Priority two is Act II, where the 2D permutation and cycle views, the six-letter theorem animations, the cyclometer and the catalogue lookup port `rejewski.py` and jfbouch's worked example into the first interactive Rejewski explainer. Priority three is Act III, whose distinguishing artefact is the 8-then-26-wire bombe state view with the diagonal-board toggle, with CyberChef's search core behind it and Virtual Bombe or owenautosport linked for drums. Priority four is the capstone and the side quests (Zygalski light table, Herivel square, cillies, hill-climb, M4/Shark), each of which is a "watch it converge" module needing little gating. The audience is adult and self-taught, so faded worked examples belong in Acts I–II and problem-first gates from II.6 onward, and the code the learner writes should never exceed the nine mechanism-defining functions named in the chapter table plus `enigma_keypress` assembled from provided parts.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the brief: the hard part is not the 3D machine, which two hobbyists and a museum volunteer have already built in three.js, nor the accurate engine, which fits in 150 lines once the double step and ring formula are pinned by vectors, but the gate design. The tools that exist either hide the circuit to preserve fidelity or expose it in tables that nobody finds engaging, and the only explainer with mass reach is a video that cannot ask the viewer anything. The pedagogical literature says the most valuable seconds in the course are the ones just before each animation, when the learner has to commit to a lamp, a window letter, a cycle length or a live-wire count; the tutoring literature says those same seconds are what an AI-assisted learner will skip unless the gate demands them. A course built on the pairing rule, prediction plus code rather than code alone, turns the learner's agent from a way to bypass the lesson into a tool for exploring it, which is the honest version of "coding with agents".

Two artefacts would be new to the field and should be treated as the project's contribution rather than its overhead: a live wire-state view of the bombe's test register, which every explainer describes and no simulator shows, and a browser implementation of Rejewski's cycle attack, which Casselman found nobody explained well online in 2009 and which still has no interactive form in 2026. Both are small, both are checkable by the same engine that runs the 3D machine, and both give the story its two climaxes, the plugboard dropping out of the cycle structure and the loop that implies itself. Build those well and the rest of the course is a well-lit path between them.
