## Key Question 1: Exact machine specification (Enigma I / M3 / M4)

### Takeaway
The Wehrmacht Enigma I and Kriegsmarine M3 share rotors I–V, an identity entry wheel, and reflectors B/C; M3 adds double-notched rotors VI–VIII, and M4 adds a non-stepping Beta/Gamma "Greek" rotor with thin reflectors. All wirings below are triple-cross-checked (Cryptomuseum, Rijmenants, Wikipedia) and reproduce every published test vector, including the double-stepping sequence ADU→ADV→AEW→BFX (the brief's "ADU→AEV→BFW" is off by one).

### Cited Findings

**Entry wheel (ETW / Eintrittswalze)**
- Enigma I ETW wiring is the identity: "ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ" — [Cryptomuseum wiring tables](https://www.cryptomuseum.com/crypto/enigma/wiring.htm); confirmed "ETW: ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ" — [Wikipedia, Enigma rotor details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_rotor_details); "Entry = ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ" — [Rijmenants, Enigma technical details](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm).
- The commercial Enigma's ETW followed keyboard order (Q→A, W→B, E→C...: "JWULCMNOHPQZYXIRADKEGVBTSF"), and Rejewski's guess in December 1932/January 1933 that the military ETW was instead the identity permutation is what unlocked his equations: "I assumed that ... maybe they were connected in alphabetical order; that is, the permutation caused by the entry drum was an identity ... The hypothesis turned out to be correct." — [Christensen, Mathematics Magazine 80(4) 2007, quoting Rejewski](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)

**Signal path and order of operations**
- "The current flows from the battery through the depressed bi-directional A key to the plugboard ... via the fixed entry wheel through the scrambled wirings of the three rotors to arrive at the reflector ... The reflector returns the signal via a different path back through the rotors." and crucially "depressing a key will first step the rotors and then send the current through the rotors and the light bulb." — [Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)
- Path used by Christensen with a worked letter: keyboard A → plugboard (A↔C) → right rotor → middle → left → reflector → left → middle → right → plugboard → lamp L — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Implementation order (verified): STEP rotors; c = plugboard(c); c = ETW(c) (identity); for rotor in [right, middle, left, (Greek)]: c = rotor.forward(c); c = reflector(c); for rotor in [(Greek), left, middle, right]: c = rotor.backward(c); c = ETW⁻¹(c); c = plugboard(c); light lamp.

**Ring settings (Ringstellung)**
- "Changing the position of the ring will change the position of the notch and alphabet, relative to the internal wiring ... When changing the ring setting one position, the encryption is shifted one position. The rotor turnover point remains at the same letter, because the notch is located at the alphabet ring. The ring setting should not be confused with the rotor position." — [Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)
- Worked example: "rotor I in A-position normally encodes A into E, with ring setting offset B-02 it will be encoded into K." and "rotors I, II, III with wide B-reflector, all ring settings in B-position, start AAA, typing AAAAA produces EWTYX." — [Wikipedia, Enigma rotor details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_rotor_details). Both verified (verify.py checks 2, 3).
- Implementation formula (verified against all vectors): with rotor position p and ring r (both 0-based), offset o = (p − r) mod 26; forward: out = (W[(in + o) mod 26] − o) mod 26; backward: same with W⁻¹. Turnover test uses the WINDOW letter (p), not p − r, because the notch is fixed to the ring.

**Stepping and the double-step anomaly**
- "The middle rotor will advance on the next step of the first rotor a second time in a row, if the middle rotor is in its own turnover position. This is called the double-step." Example with rotors III II I (left→right): "KDO KDP, KDQ, KER, LFS, LFT, LFU…" — "stepping from Q to R advances the middle rotor, and on the next step that middle rotor steps again, advancing the third rotor also." — [Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm). Verified (check 4b).
- Wikipedia's canonical sequence for rotors I II III: "Normal sequence: AAU, AAV, ABW, ABX." and "Double step sequence: ADU, ADV, AEW (right rotor steps, advances middle rotor now in E-notch), BFX (normal, double, normal stepping), BFY." — [Wikipedia, Enigma rotor details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_rotor_details). Verified (check 4). NOTE: the sequence quoted in the brief ("ADU→AEV→BFW") is wrong by one step; after one keypress from ADU the window shows ADV (middle rotor carries only when the right rotor moves V→W).
- Period: "With three wheels and only single notches in the first and second wheels, the machine had a period of 26×25×26 = 16,900 (not 26×26×26, because of double-stepping)." — [Wikipedia, Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine); same figure in [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf).
- Verified stepping rule (verify.py): `stepMiddle = right.atTurnover() OR middle.atTurnover(); stepLeft = middle.atTurnover(); right.step(); if stepMiddle: middle.step(); if stepLeft: left.step()`. For M4 the Greek rotor never steps.

**Plugboard (Steckerbrett)**
- Self-inverse pairwise swap applied on the way in and on the way out; "Initially using 6 pairs, the standard evolved to use 10 pairs by WWII, with up to 13 possible." — [Wikipedia, Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine)
- "When the Polish mathematicians began their attack on Enigma, six plugs were in use ... 14 letters were fixed by the plugboard permutation." — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- "On 1 January 1939, the Germans increased the number of plugboard connections from between five and eight to between seven and ten" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Reciprocity and no-self-encryption: "No letter ever encrypted to itself. This was a severe cryptological flaw that was subsequently exploited by codebreakers." — [Wikipedia, Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine); "every Enigma permutation is a product of 13 disjoint transpositions ... Every Enigma permutation is self-reciprocal ... it also guarantees that no letter can be enciphered as itself" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf). Verified over 2,000 consecutive positions × 26 letters (check 11).

## Key Question 2: Key-space arithmetic

### Takeaway
60 wheel orders × 17,576 rotor positions × 150,738,274,937,250 plugboard settings (10 leads) = 158,962,555,217,826,360,000 ≈ 1.59 × 10²⁰; multiplying by the 676 cryptographically relevant ring settings gives ≈ 1.07 × 10²³ (Rijmenants' figure), and the 1932 machine (6 orders, 6 plugs) had ≈ 7.16 × 10¹⁸ effective keys.

### Cited Findings
- "The 3-rotor scrambler could be set in 26 × 26 × 26 = 17 576 ways, and the 4-rotor scrambler in 26 × 17 576 = 456 976 ways." and "With L leads on the plugboard, the number of ways that pairs of letters could be interchanged was 26!/((26−2L)!·L!·2^L)" ... "With L=6, the number of combinations was 100 391 791 500 (100 billion) and with ten leads, it was 150 738 274 937 250 (151 trillion)." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- "To select 3 rotors out of a possible 5, there are 60 combinations (5 x 4 x 3)"; "only the rings of the most-right and middle rotor affect the calculation. This gives 676 ring combinations (26 x 26)"; "60 x 17,576 x 676 x 150,738,274,937,250 = 107,458,687,327,250,619,360,000 or 1.07 x 10²³ ... comparable with a 77 bit key." M4: "336 rotor combinations (8 x 7 x 6) ... 456,976 positions ... 336 x 2 x 456,976 x 676 x 150,738,274,937,250 = 31,291,969,749,695,380,357,632,000 or 3.1 x 10²⁵". — [Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)
- Full plugboard table n=0..13: 1; 325; 44,850; 3,453,450; 164,038,875; 5,019,589,575; 100,391,791,500; 1,305,093,289,500; 10,767,019,638,375; 53,835,098,191,875; 150,738,274,937,250; 205,552,193,096,250; 102,776,096,548,125; 7,905,853,580,625 (note the maximum is at 11 plugs, not 13) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- 1932-era effective key count: "100,391,791,500 × 6 × 676 × 17576 = 7,156,755,732,750,624,000"; and "Only the positions of the notches on the right-hand and middle rotors contributed to the cryptographic security ... 26² = 676 possible ring settings." — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Number of possible Enigma alphabets (13 disjoint transpositions): 7,905,853,580,625, "many fewer than the 26! possible simple substitution permutations" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- "The plugboard contributed more cryptographic strength than an extra rotor, as it had 150 trillion possible settings." — [Wikipedia, Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine)
- Computed and verified (verify.py): 60 × 17,576 × 150,738,274,937,250 = 158,962,555,217,826,360,000 = 1.590 × 10²⁰; × 676 = 107,458,687,327,250,619,360,000 = 1.075 × 10²³ (matches Rijmenants exactly).

### Inferences
- Why rings "don't add" in the 1.59×10²⁰ figure: for a message of a given length, rotor position and ring setting are confounded except through when carries occur; only the middle and right rings change the carry timing, so 26² is the honest extra factor and the left ring adds nothing. Both conventions (with/without 676) appear in reputable sources; the app should show both and say why.
- Turing/Rejewski attacks never searched the plugboard space: the 1.59×10²⁰ figure is misleading as a measure of work because the cyclometer/catalog reduced the problem to 105,456 (6 × 17,576) and the bombe to 60 × 17,576 = 1,054,560 scrambler states (see Q4–Q5).

### Gaps
- None material.

## Key Question 3: Indicator procedures (why the Polish attack depended on the doubled key)

### Takeaway
Until 15 Sept 1938 every operator enciphered his 3-letter message key twice at a network-wide daily Grundstellung, so the first six letters of every message were produced by the same six permutations A–F; the Sept 1938 change let operators choose the Grundstellung (killing the catalog but leaving doubling, hence Zygalski sheets and bombas), and 1 May 1940 abolished doubling altogether (killing the sheets).

### Cited Findings
- Pre-1940 Army procedure: "The operator chose a random message key and, with the rotors in the daily start position, he encrypted the message key twice to exclude errors." Example: key GHK enciphered twice at the daily Grundstellung gives "XMC FZQ"; the receiver decrypts XMC FZQ → GHK and sets GHK. "The message key is encrypted twice, resulting in a relation between first and fourth, second and fifth, and third and sixth character." — [Rijmenants, Enigma message procedures](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmaproc.htm)
- The 1930 manual message shows the same structure concretely: Grundstellung FOL, indicator PKPJXI, message key ABL — [CryptoCellar](https://cryptocellar.org/enigma/e-message-1930.html)
- "15 September 1938: The operator selected his initial position to encrypt the message key, and sent the initial position in clear followed by the enciphered message key." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- "on September 15, 1938, the Germans changed entirely the procedure for enciphering message keys," rendering the card catalogue "completely useless." — [Wikipedia, Cyclometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclometer)
- "1 May 1940: The Germans ... discontinuing the duplication of the enciphered message key." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); "In May 1940, the Germans once again completely changed the procedure for enciphering message keys ... As a result, Zygalski's sheets were of no use." — [Wikipedia, Zygalski sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygalski_sheets)
- From 1940 (single encipherment): operator picks Grundstellung WZA and message key SXT, enciphers SXT at WZA → RSK, sends "WZA RSK", enciphers the body at SXT; full preamble example "1230 = 3tle = 1tl = 180 = WZA RSK = FDJKM LQWOR MDFVO..." — [Rijmenants procedures](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmaproc.htm)
- Key-sheet contents: "Walzenlage [rotor choice/order], Ringstellung [ring setting], Steckerverbindungen [plugboard connections], Kenngruppen [identification groups]." — [Rijmenants procedures](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmaproc.htm)
- Why doubling matters mathematically: "Rejewski would not have known the message setting NKU, but he would have known that the first letter ... was changed to J by permutation A and changed to Q by permutation D ... Because Enigma ciphers are self-reciprocal, we know that AD: J → ? → Q." — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); "If we have a sufficient number of messages (about eighty) for a given day, then, in general, all the letters of the alphabet will occur in all six places" (Rejewski) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); Vábek cites "about 80 messages ... or even 60 messages" — [Vábek, WDS'06](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)
- Naval procedure (never doubled in the same way; defeated the Poles): from 1 May 1937 the Navy "used the same sort of setting sheet but, importantly, it included the ground key for a period of two, sometimes three days. The message setting was concealed in the indicator by selecting a trigram from a book (the Kenngruppenbuch, or K-Book) and performing a bigram substitution on it. This defeated the Poles" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); "Naval procedures were 'far more complex and elaborate' using separate internal and external key sheets, Kenngruppenbuch indicator books, and Doppelbuchstabentauschtafel (bigram tables)" — [Rijmenants procedures](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmaproc.htm)

### Inferences
- For story mode, the three procedure eras map neatly onto three Polish/British tools: (1) daily Grundstellung + doubling → characteristics/cyclometer/catalog; (2) operator Grundstellung + doubling → Zygalski sheets and bomba (both need only "females", not a common Grundstellung); (3) no doubling → cribs and the Turing–Welchman bombe (which never used the indicator at all).

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from fetched text the commonly cited 1 Feb 1936 (rotor order changed monthly) and 1 Oct 1936 (daily rotor order, 5–8 plugs) changes; the Wikipedia wikitext I retrieved mentions "between five and eight" plugs only in the context of the 1 Jan 1939 change. Treat the 1936 dates as unverified.

## Key Question 4: Rejewski's mathematics (permutations A–F, characteristics, cyclometer, sheets, bomba, wiring recovery)

### Takeaway
Rejewski turned the doubled indicator into three observable permutations AD, BE, CF whose cycle structure is invariant under the plugboard (conjugation), reducing 105,456 = 6 × 17,576 rotor states to a catalog lookup; with Schmidt's key sheets he then solved six equations for the fast-rotor wiring; after Sept 1938 the Poles exploited "females" with Zygalski sheets and six bombas until the Dec 1938/Jan 1939 changes outran their resources.

### Cited Findings

**The six permutations and the products (Rejewski's notation, composition LEFT-TO-RIGHT)**
- "Rejewski composes his permutations from left to right, and we will follow his notation." First-letter permutation A = S P N M L R L⁻¹ M⁻¹ N⁻¹ P⁻¹ S⁻¹ = (SPNML) R (SPNML)⁻¹; second letter uses P²; "an Enigma permutation is always a conjugate of the reflector" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf). S = plugboard, N = right (fast) rotor, M = middle, L = left, R = reflector, P = (abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz) the cyclic shift modelling the fast rotor's motion.
- Full system (with entry permutation H, identity on the military machine): A = S H P N P⁻¹ M L R L⁻¹ M⁻¹ P N⁻¹ P⁻¹ H⁻¹ S⁻¹, ..., F = S H P⁶ N P⁻⁶ M L R L⁻¹ M⁻¹ P⁶ N⁻¹ P⁻⁶ H⁻¹ S⁻¹ — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)
- Observable products: for indicator "dmq vbf", "xA = d and also xD = v. But because also dA = x (self-reciprocity) ... dAD = v" — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)

**Worked example (reproducible in-app): 65 indicators → AD, BE, CF**
- Indicator list (from Bauer via Christensen): AUQ AMN, IND JHU, PVJ FEG, SJM SPO, WTM RAO, BNH CHL, JWF MIC, QGA LYB, BCT CGJ, CIK BZT, KHB XJV, RJL WPX, SUG SMF, WKI RKK, DDB VDV, XRS GNM, EJP IPS, LDR HDE, TMN EBY, FBR KLE, XOI GUK, GPB ZSV, MAW UXP, RFC WQQ, TAA EXB, XYW GCP, HNO THD, SYX SCW, USE NWH, YPC OSQ, NXD QTU, VII PZK, HXV TTI, ZZY YRA, IKG JKF, NLU QFZ, VQZ PVR, ZEF YOC, OBU DLZ, ZSJ YWG (plus repeats) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Resulting AD as a substitution: a..z → "ACBVIKZTJMXHUQDFLWSENPRGOY"; "AD = (a)(bc)(dvpfkxgzyo)(eijmunqlht)(rw)(s), and the lengths of the cycles are 10 10 2 2 1 1"; "BE = (axt)(blfqveoum)(cgy)(d)(hjpswizrn)(k)" lengths 9 9 3 3 1 1; "CF = (abviktjgfcqny)(duzrehlxwpsmo)" lengths 13 13 — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf). The AD decomposition was recomputed from the substitution string in verify.py and matches.
- The resulting factorizations (Rejewski's example): A = (as)(br)(cw)(di)(ev)(fh)(gn)(jo)(kl)(my)(pt)(qx)(uz); B = (ay)(bj)(ct)(dk)(ei)(fn)(gx)(hl)(mp)(ow)(qr)(su)(vz); C = (ax)(bl)(cm)(dg)(ei)(fo)(hv)(ju)(kr)(np)(qs)(tz)(wy); D = (as)(bw)(cr)(dj)(ep)(ft)(gq)(hk)(iv)(lx)(mo)(nz)(uy); E = (ac)(bp)(dk)(ez)(fh)(gt)(io)(jl)(ms)(nq)(rv)(uw)(xy); F = (aw)(bx)(co)(df)(ek)(gu)(hi)(jz)(lv)(mq)(ns)(py)(rt) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Counting the factorizations of AD: 1-cycles (a)(s) force (as) in both A and D; the two 2-cycles give 2 orderings; the two 10-cycles give 10 orderings; "So there are 1 × 2 × 10 = 20 possible factorizations of AD." Operators' non-random key habits ("initials, patterns in rows or diagonals of the keyboard") pick the right one — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)

**The invariance theorem ("the theorem that won World War II")**
- "AD = S P₁ P₄ S⁻¹ where P₁ = PNMLRL⁻¹M⁻¹N⁻¹P⁻¹ and P₄ = P⁴NMLRL⁻¹M⁻¹N⁻¹P⁻⁴ are each determined only by the rotor order and groundsetting. Because of the theorem ... that the disjoint cycle structure of a permutation and a conjugate of the permutation are the same, the disjoint cycle structure of AD is the same as it would be if there were no plugboard; the effect of the plugboard has been nullified!" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- "two permutations are conjugate if and only if they have the same cycle structure" — which cryptologist A. Deavours described as "the theorem that won World War II" — [Wikipedia, Marian Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski)
- Lemma 5 (with recipe): "Two permutations X and Y are conjugated (i.e. there exists Z, such that X = ZYZ⁻¹) if and only if they are of the same type"; "It is enough to subscribe the cycles of the permutation Y beneath the cycles of the same length of the permutation X and we can directly see the mapping Z"; the number of such Z is ∏ i^{k_i}·k_i! — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)

**Rejewski's theorem on products of transpositions (why cycles come in equal-length pairs)**
- THEOREM 1: "If two permutations of the same degree consist solely of disjoint transpositions, then their product will consist of disjoint cycles of the same length in even numbers." Proof sketch: X = (a₁a₂)(a₃a₄)…(a₂ₖ₋₁a₂ₖ), Y = (a₂a₃)(a₄a₅)…(a₂ₖa₁), then XY = (a₁a₃a₅…a₂ₖ₋₁)(a₂ₖa₂ₖ₋₂…a₄a₂). THEOREM 2 (converse): such a permutation "may be regarded as a product of two permutations, each consisting solely of disjoint transpositions." THEOREM 3: "Letters entering into one and the same transposition of permutation X or Y, enter always into two different cycles of the permutation XY." THEOREM 4: "If two letters found in two different cycles of the same length of the permutation XY belong to the same transposition, then the letters adjacent to them (one to the right, the other to the left) also belong to the same transposition." — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); "Theorem 6. For a permutation A there exist two alphabets X and Y such that A = XY, if and only if A contains an even number of cycles of each length." — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)
- Tiny animatable example (computed in verify.py, left-to-right composition): X = (ab)(cd)(ef), Y = (bc)(de)(fa) ⇒ XY = (ace)(bfd): one 6-letter "chain" of transpositions produces two 3-cycles running in opposite directions.

**Characteristics, cyclometer and the card catalogue**
- "Rejewski uses in his texts the expressions characteristic of a given day only for three permutations AD, BE and CF." — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)
- "The product 6 × 17576 = 105,456 corresponds to the number of possible rotor orders and groundsettings" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); "There were only 105,456 possible rotor settings ... There are roughly 1 million possible cycle-length combinations and only 105,456 starting positions." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Cyclometer operation: two rotor sets in the same order, "the first set of rotors was set to a groundsetting (e.g., NKU), and the second set of rotors was stepped three positions beyond the groundsetting (NKX). So, the rotors were set up as if they were permutations A and D." Current applied at A cycles through both banks until it returns; a 4-cycle (ajqe) lights eight lamps (A,J,Q,E and G,N,H,S — the paired cycle); then advance both sets one step for BE, again for CF; "The eight lamps that are lit told the Poles that AD contained two 4-cycles." — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Rejewski: "This job took a long time, over a year ... Once all six card catalogues [one for each of the six possible orders of the rotors] were ready, though, obtaining a daily key was usually a matter of ten to twenty minutes. The card told the drum positions ..., the box from which the card had been taken told the drum sequence ..., and the permutation S ... was obtained by comparing the letters of the cycles of permutations AD, BE, CF" — [Christensen quoting Rejewski](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); "some fifteen minutes" and "1934 or 1935" for the cyclometer — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); "Mid-1930s: Cyclometer invented" — [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski)
- The catalogue-to-setting map is many-to-one: "Several rotor orders and groundsettings can result in the same characteristic"; Carter's modern reconstruction: "In bad cases, the number of possibilities given by the catalogue would have been daunting ... For the majority of patterns however, the starting positions would have been found immediately" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Grill method (pre-cyclometer): "The Poles would then use the grill method to determine the rightmost rotor and its position ... but that permutation only swapped six pairs of letters — not enough to disrupt the search. The grill method also determined the plugboard wiring." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

**Recovering the rotor wiring with Schmidt's documents**
- "quite unexpectedly on 9 December 1932, at just the right moment, I was given a photocopy of two tables of daily keys for September and October 1932 ... Since the key tables also contained the daily changes in the commutator connections, I could now regard S as known" (Rejewski) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- "The French had purchased the information ... from the German traitor Hans Thilo Schmidt (code name Asché). In 1932, German Enigma procedures called for changing the order of the three rotors once per quarter. Because September is in one quarter of the year and October is in the next, the information from Schmidt provided the plugboard connections when two different rotors were in the right-hand rotor location." — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Solution outline (with Q = M L R L⁻¹ M⁻¹ constant across the six letters because the middle rotor is assumed not to turn): move S and P to the left: U = P⁻¹S⁻¹ASP = N P⁻¹ Q P N⁻¹, V = P⁻²S⁻¹BSP² = N P⁻² Q P² N⁻¹, W, X similarly; form UV, VW, WX, which are all conjugates of Q P⁻¹ Q P and hence share cycle type; eliminate: VW = (NPN⁻¹)⁻¹ (UV) (NPN⁻¹) and WX = (NPN⁻¹)⁻¹ (VW) (NPN⁻¹); the unique common solution in Rejewski's example is NPN⁻¹ = (ayuricxqmgovskedzplfwtnjhb); subscribing P beneath it in 26 ways gives 26 candidate N differing only by rotation, e.g. N: a..z → "azfpotjyexnsiwkrhdmvclugbq" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); same derivation with equations (4)–(6) — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)
- Example data from Rejewski: S = (ap)(bl)(cz)(fh)(jk)(qu); U = (ax)(bu)(ck)(dr)(ej)(fw)(gi)(lp)(ms)(nz)(oh)(qt)(vy); V = (ar)(bv)(co)(dh)(fl)(gk)(iz)(jp)(mn)(qy)(su)(tw)(xe); W = (as)(bz)(cp)(dq)(eo)(fw)(gj)(hl)(iy)(kr)(mu)(nt)(vx); X = (ap)(bf)(cu)(dv)(ei)(gr)(ho)(jn)(ky)(lx)(mz)(qs)(tw); UV = (aepftybsnikod)(rhcgzmuvqwljx); VW = (akjcevzydlwnu)(smtfhqibxopgr); WX = (aqvloikgnwbmc)(puzftjryehxds) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- How the third rotor and reflector were found is NOT fully documented by Rejewski; his four accounts "differ from each other or in some cases even slightly contradict each other" — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)
- Outcome: "By December, 1932, Rejewski knew the wiring of the Enigma rotors ... By the middle of January, 1933, the Poles were able to read Enigma messages." (Welchman: "After only a month of continuous and highly concentrated effort") — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); "By end of 1932: All three rotor wirings and reflector recovered" — [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski)

**Zygalski sheets (females)**
- "Sometimes (about one message in eight) one of the repeated letters in the message key enciphered to the same letter on both occasions. These occurrences were called samiczki (in English, females ...). If the first six letters of the ciphertext were SZVSIK, this would be termed a 1–4 female; if WHOEHS, a 2–5 female; and if ASWCRW, a 3–6 female. The method was called Netz (from Netzverfahren, 'net method')" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- "Each sheet related to the starting position of the left (slowest-moving) rotor. The 26 × 26 matrix represented the 676 possible starting positions of the middle and right rotors and was duplicated horizontally and vertically: a–z, a–y." 26 sheets per wheel order (156 for six orders; "60 sets" after Dec 1938); "about 1000 holes" per sheet; "When the sheets were superposed and moved in the proper sequence ... the number of visible apertures gradually decreased." Invented "about October 1938"; British set completed "7 January 1940"; first wartime decrypt "17 January 1940" — [Wikipedia, Zygalski sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygalski_sheets); "There was a set of 26 of these sheets for each of the six possible sequences wheel orders." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

**Bomba kryptologiczna**
- "designed around October 1938" by Rejewski; "by mid-November 1938, the bombs were ready, and the reconstructing of daily keys now took about two hours"; "Each cryptologic bomb ... essentially constituted an electrically powered aggregate of six Enigmas. It took the place of about one hundred workers"; "six were built in Warsaw"; it "relied on the fact that the plug connections ... did not change all the letters", i.e., it needed unsteckered letters in the females; named "for lack of a better idea", most credibly from "the characteristic muffled noise that it produced" — [Wikipedia, Bomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomba_(cryptography)); "Each machine contained six sets of Enigma rotors for the six positions of the repeated three-letter key" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); "November 15, 1938: Six bomba machines operational" — [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski)
- Defeat: rotors IV and V (15 Dec 1938) "raised the number of possible sequences of drums from 6 to 60", requiring sixty bombas; the 1 Jan 1939 increase to 7–10 plugs made the unsteckered-letter assumption fail — [Wikipedia, Bomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomba_(cryptography)); Rejewski nevertheless "by about the turn of the year ... had worked out the wirings of the two new rotors" because the SD network kept the old indicator procedure — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

### Inferences
- Count of possible characteristics: each of AD, BE, CF has cycles in equal-length pairs, so its cycle type is a partition of 13 doubled; p(13) = 101 (computed), hence 101³ = 1,030,301 possible (AD, BE, CF) signatures — consistent with Wikipedia's "roughly 1 million" and Christensen's remark that the map from 105,456 settings to characteristics is many-to-one. This is a good number to animate: 105,456 settings hashed into ~1.03 million buckets, so most buckets are empty or tiny.
- The Poles' three tools each attack a different invariant: catalog (cycle lengths, needs common Grundstellung), sheets (positions of females, needs only doubling), bomba (females at the same letter in three messages, needs doubling plus unsteckered letters). This ordering is the natural story-mode progression.

### Gaps
- Rejewski's original 1980 paper ("An Application of the Theory of Permutations in Breaking the Enigma Cipher", Applicationes Mathematicae 16(4):543–559) is indexed at [EuDML](https://eudml.org/doc/264403) but I could not fetch full text; all equations above are quoted via Christensen and Vábek, who cite it directly. The 1981 Annals of the History of Computing paper was not fetched (paywalled).
- No surviving copy of the Polish card catalogue exists ("Apparently no copies of their catalogue still exist" — Christensen), so the exact indexing scheme is unknown.
- How many bombas/sheets were used per day and exact bomba run times beyond "about two hours" are not in the fetched sources.

## Key Question 5: The Turing–Welchman bombe (cribs, menus, loops, diagonal board, stops)

### Takeaway
The bombe tests, for each of 17,576 scrambler positions per wheel order, a single plugboard hypothesis for one letter of a crib-derived menu and lets electrical propagation through the menu's loops (and Welchman's diagonal board) generate contradictions; a run took about 20 minutes per wheel order, and Turing's stop analysis dictated crib length versus loop count.

### Cited Findings

**Cribs and crib placement**
- "the reflector in the scrambler prevented a letter from being enciphered as itself", so cribs are slid along the ciphertext and any alignment with a same-letter coincidence (a "crash") is eliminated — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe)
- Typical cribs: "Keine besonderen Ereignisse", "An die Gruppe", weather reports rendered "WEUBYYNULLSEQSNULLNULL" (WEUB = Wetterübersicht, YY separator, SEQS = sechs); re-encipherments of already-broken messages ("kisses") — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis of the Enigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Stecker-partner illustration: "if the crib was the word WETTER ... and a possible decrypt before the plugboard settings had been discovered, was TEWWER, it is easy to see that T with W are stecker partners." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

**Menu example (reproducible; verified for crashes and loops in verify.py)**
- Ciphertext W S N P N L K L S T C S over crib A T T A C K A T D A W N, positions 1–12; "The graph identified three loops: 'ATLK,' 'TNS,' and 'TAWCN.'" — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe). Verified: no crashes; edges (A–W@1, T–S@2, T–N@3, A–P@4, C–N@5, K–L@6, A–K@7, T–L@8, D–S@9, A–T@10, W–C@11, N–S@12); independent loops: {2,3,12} = T-N-S, {6,7,8,10} = A-T-L-K, {1,3,5,10,11} = A-W-C-N-T.
- Tony Sale's example: ciphertext "JYCQRPWYDEMCJMRSR" against crib "SPRUCHNUMMERXEINS"; "C->R at offset c, e and l from the start and M->E at j, k and n"; loop test: "The test for a loop of possible Steckers at a particular drum order and rotor position is to see if either only one or 25 of the lamps are lit. If all 26 lamps light then this position can be rejected" — [Tony Sale, The Turing Bombe](https://www.codesandciphers.org.uk/virtualbp/tbombe/tbombe.htm)

**Hypothesis-rejection logic**
- "The process assumes one plugboard interconnection for the bombe to test"; e.g., if P(A)=Y then via the crib "P(T) = S₁₀(Y) = Q" etc.; when the chain returns "P(A) = N" contradicting Y, the setting is eliminated; "Stops occur when no contradiction arises." — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe)
- Tony Sale: "R Steckered to S1 enciphers to S2 Steckered to N at g; N Steckered to S2 enciphers to S3 Steckered to S at p; S Steckered to S3 enciphers to S1 Steckered to R at q"; "a voltage placed onto the S1 input of the first opened out Enigma ... will come out on the S2 terminal" and cycles the loop — [Tony Sale](https://www.codesandciphers.org.uk/virtualbp/tbombe/tbombe.htm)
- Menu specifies "the connections of the cables of the patch panels on the back of the machine, and a particular letter whose stecker partner was sought" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Post-stop checking: stops with plugboard contradictions were discarded; "The other plugboard connections and the settings of the alphabet rings would then be worked out before the scrambler positions at the possible true stops were tried out on Typex machines that had been adapted to mimic Enigmas." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); "tested on a Typex machine that had been modified to replicate an Enigma" — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe)

**Welchman's diagonal board**
- "Early in 1940, before the first pre-production bombe was delivered," Welchman "showed him an idea to increase its effectiveness. It exploited the reciprocity in plugboard connections" — the diagonal board "was subsequently incorporated to great effect in all the bombes." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); "if B is Steckered to G then G is also Steckered to B" — [Tony Sale](https://www.codesandciphers.org.uk/virtualbp/tbombe/tbombe.htm); it "vastly increased its efficiency" — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe)

**Turing's stop analysis (why loops matter)**
- "It was adopted as standard practice only to use menus that were estimated to produce no more than four stops per wheel order. This allowed an 8-letter crib for a 3-closure menu, an 11-letter crib for a 2-closure menu, and a 14-letter crib for a menu with only one closure. If there was no closure, at least 16 letters were required in the crib. The longer the crib, however, the more likely it was that turn-over of the middle rotor would have occurred." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Expected stops per wheel order (Turing's table): 3 loops: 2.2 (8 letters), 0.04 (12), <0.01 (16); 1 loop: 1500, 31, 0.04; 0 loops: 40000, 820, 1.0 — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe)

**Hardware and timeline**
- "36 Enigma equivalents, each with three drums" in "three groups of 12 triplets" (= 108 drums); "26 × 26 × 26 = 17,576" positions per wheel order; "about 20 minutes" per run at 50.4 rpm on early machines, later 120 rpm — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe)
- "Harold 'Doc' Keen of the British Tabulating Machine Company ... was the engineer who turned Turing's ideas into a working machine." "The first bombe was named Victory and was delivered to Bletchley Park on 18 March 1940." The next, with the diagonal board, "was delivered on 8 August 1940. It was referred to as a spider bombe and was named Agnus Dei" (later Agnes/Aggie); "only five bombes being in use in June 1941, 15 by the year end, 30 by September 1942, 49 by January 1943 but eventually 210 at the end of the war." "By 1945 there were some 2,000 Wrens operating the bombes." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma). (The Bombe article's table gives 155 three-rotor bombes in May 1945; the 210 figure includes other types — sources differ in what they count.)
- US Navy 4-rotor bombe (NCR, Dayton; Joseph Desch): "16 four-rotor Enigma-analogues", drums at "1,725 rpm, 34 times the speed of the early British bombes", "about 20 minutes" for a 4-rotor run and "about 50 seconds" for 3-rotor; "180" four-rotor bombes available by May 1945 (UK+US) — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe); Turing seconded to Washington December 1942 to advise on them — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- "Consecutive Stecker Knock Out" fitted to 40 bombes for networks forbidding adjacent-letter plugs — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

**Operator-error shortcuts**
- Herivel tip: "Herivel had an insight in February 1940 that some lazy German code clerks might give away the Enigma's ring settings (Ringstellung) in their first message of the day" by using "whatever rotor setting was currently showing" after setting the rings; the Herivel square reduced possibilities "from 17,576 to a small set of possibilities, perhaps 6 to 30"; became vital after "In May 1940, the Germans stopped the doubly-enciphered keys" — first success "on 22 May a Luftwaffe message sent on 20 May was decoded" — [Wikipedia, Herivel tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herivel_tip)
- Cillies: "easily guessed keys such as AAA or BBB, or sequences that reflected the layout of the Enigma keyboard"; "with multi-part messages, the operator would not enter a key for a subsequent part of a message, merely leaving the rotors as they were at the end of the previous part" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Banburismus: "The aim of Banburismus was to reduce the time required of the electromechanical Bombe machines by identifying the most likely right-hand and middle wheels"; coincidence rate "1 in 17 (5.9%)" for German naval text vs 1 in 26 random; messages "punched onto thin cards about 250 millimetres (9.8 in) high" printed in Banbury, overlaid on a light-box; Turing's "ban" and "deciban"; Hut 8 ran it "continuously for two years, stopping only in 1943" — [Wikipedia, Banburismus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banburismus); "Banburismus was used until July 1943"; it extended "the clock method invented by the Polish cryptanalyst Jerzy Różycki" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

### Inferences
- For an in-app bombe animation, the ATTACKATDAWN/WSNPNLKLSTCS menu is the best choice: it is short (12 letters), has three independent closures (meeting Turing's "8-letter crib with 3 closures" threshold), no crashes, and is a documented example; the app can drive its own verified Enigma core as the 12 "scramblers" at offsets 1..12 from a trial position.
- The "1 or 25 lamps lit vs 26" test (Tony Sale) is the crisp visual: a wrong hypothesis floods all 26 wires of the test register; a right one leaves either the single true Stecker wire live (if the hypothesis was right) or all except the true one (if it was wrong but the position is right).

## Key Question 6: Chronology, people, turning points, and myths

### Takeaway
The verified chronology runs from Scherbius's 23 Feb 1918 patent through Rejewski's December 1932 wiring recovery, the 25–27 July 1939 Pyry handover, the March/August 1940 bombes, the Feb–Dec 1942 Shark blackout, and the 1974 lifting of secrecy; the main myths to correct are "Turing alone", "Colossus broke Enigma", "the Americans captured U-110", and the wrong double-step sequence.

### Cited Findings

**Chronology (dated, sourced)**
- 23 Feb 1918: Scherbius files his cipher-machine patent; 1923 commercial marketing; 1926 Reichsmarine adoption; 1928 Reichswehr version; 1930 Enigma I in service; "Approximately 40,000 Enigma machines were constructed" — [Wikipedia, Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine)
- 1927: UK buys a commercial Enigma; April 1937 Dilly Knox's first decrypt (unsteckered Spanish Civil War traffic) using "buttoning up" and "rodding" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Early 1929: Poznań cryptology course; 1 Sept 1932 Rejewski, Zygalski, Różycki hired in Warsaw; late Oct/Nov 1932 Rejewski tasked with Enigma; 9 Dec 1932 Schmidt's key tables (Sept–Oct 1932) delivered via Bertrand; by end 1932 wirings recovered — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski); [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- 1934/35 cyclometer; catalogue took "over a year"; 1/2 Nov 1937 reflector A→B forces a redo ("probably somewhat less than a year's time") — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- 15 Sept 1938 indicator change; Oct 1938 Zygalski sheets and bomba design; mid-Nov 1938 six bombas; 15 Dec 1938 rotors IV, V; 1 Jan 1939 7–10 plugs — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); [Wikipedia, Bomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomba_(cryptography)); [Wikipedia, Zygalski sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygalski_sheets)
- Pyry meeting: "At a conference near Warsaw on 26 and 27 July 1939, the Poles revealed to the French and British that they had broken Enigma and pledged to give each a Polish-reconstructed Enigma ... including Zygalski's perforated sheets and Rejewski's cryptologic bomb ... In return, the British pledged to prepare two full sets of Zygalski sheets for all 60 possible wheel orders." In August two Enigma doubles went to Paris; Bertrand handed one to Stewart Menzies at Victoria Station — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma). Other pages date it "25 July 1939" — [Wikipedia, Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_(cryptography)); [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski). (The meeting is variously dated 25, 26 or 26–27 July; primary account is Denniston's, ed. Erskine, Cryptologia 30(4) 2006.)
- Knox's first question at Pyry was the entry-drum wiring; Rejewski: "Knox was furious when he learned how simple it was"; Twinn: "It was such an obvious thing to do ... that nobody, not Dilly Knox, not Alan Turing, ever thought it worthwhile trying." — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- 4 Sept 1939: Turing and Welchman report to Bletchley Park "the day after Britain declared war"; Turing (part-time with GC&CS since the 1938 Munich crisis) joins Knox's research section and designs the bombe in 1939 — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Sept 1939: Poles evacuated via Romania to France (PC Bruno); June 1940 to Algeria; Sept 1940 Cadix; Nov 1942 escape via Pyrenees; 3 Aug 1943 Rejewski reaches Britain (low-grade cipher work); 1946 returns to Poland; silent until 1967; dies 13 Feb 1980 — [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski)
- 7 Jan 1940 British Zygalski sheets complete; 17 Jan 1940 first wartime decrypt — [Wikipedia, Zygalski sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygalski_sheets)
- 12 Feb 1940: rotors VI and VII captured from U-33; 26 April 1940 VP2623 ("Polares") yields manuals and key sheets — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- 18 March 1940 Victory; 1 May 1940 doubling ends; 22 May 1940 first Herivel-tip break; 8 Aug 1940 Agnus Dei with diagonal board — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); [Wikipedia, Herivel tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herivel_tip)
- Turing takes naval Enigma "because 'no one else was doing anything about it and I could have it to myself'. He established Hut 8 with Peter Twinn"; Frank Birch, 21 Aug 1940: "Turing and Twinn are like people waiting for a miracle"; Ian Fleming's Operation Ruthless (Sept 1940) cancelled — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Feb 1941: Sinkov/Rosen (US Army) and Weeks/Currier (US Navy) visit Bletchley bringing a Purple replica; March 1941 first Wrens arrive as bombe operators — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- 4 March 1941 Operation Claymore: trawler Krebs captured with February keys, enabling bigram-table reconstruction by "EINS-ing"; weather ships München and Lauenburg; 9 May 1941 U-110 (HMS Bulldog, Sub-Lt David Balme; Enigma machine, books, Kurzsignale code book) — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); [Wikipedia, U-110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_submarine_U-110_(1940))
- 1 Feb 1942: M4 for Atlantic U-boats; traffic "Shark" (German Triton) vs "Dolphin"; "Solving Shark keys on 3-rotor bombes would have taken 50 to 100 times as long" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); blackout "ten months" — [Wikipedia, Hut 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_8), [Wikipedia, U-559](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_submarine_U-559); "nine months" — [Cryptomuseum M4](https://www.cryptomuseum.com/crypto/enigma/m4/index.htm)
- 30 Oct 1942: U-559 (HMS Petard; Fasson and Grazier drowned, Tommy Brown survived; posthumous George Crosses) — key sheets and the Wetterkurzschlüssel "captured ... on 29 or 30 October 1942"; the fourth rotor's "neutral position" let short weather signals be broken on 3-rotor bombes — [Wikipedia, U-559](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_submarine_U-559); [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Shark re-entered "December 12, 1942" — [Wikipedia, Enigma-M4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma-M4) (other accounts say 13 December; not verified here)
- Nov 1942 Hugh Alexander succeeds Turing as head of Hut 8; Sept 1944 Patrick Mahon — [Wikipedia, Hut 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_8)
- Dec 1942 Turing to Washington re US bombes (NCR/Desch); 1943 US 4-rotor bombes; July 1943 Banburismus retired — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Peak throughput: "By the second half of 1941 30,000 Enigma messages a month were being deciphered, rising to 90,000 a month of Enigma and Fish decrypts combined later in the war." — [Wikipedia, Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_(cryptography))
- Secrecy: "Until the mid 1970s, the thirty year rule meant that there was no official mention of Bletchley Park." "The British ban was finally lifted in 1974, the year that ... F. W. Winterbotham, published The Ultra Secret." — [Wikipedia, Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_(cryptography)); Rejewski received the Grand Cross of Polonia Restituta (2000) and an IEEE Milestone (2014) posthumously — [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski)

**Organisation**
- Hut 6: Army/Air Force Enigma; Hut 8: naval Enigma, forwarded via Hut 4 to the Admiralty OIC; Hut 3 intelligence — [Wikipedia, Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_(cryptography)); Huts 3, 6, 8 moved to Block D in Feb 1943 — [Wikipedia, Hut 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_8)

**The Polish-contribution question**
- Welchman: "Hut 6 Ultra would never have got off the ground if we had not learned from the Poles, in the nick of time, the details both of the German military version of the commercial Enigma machine, and of the operating procedures that were in use." Calvocoressi: "According to the best qualified judges it accelerated the breaking of Enigma by perhaps a year. The British did not adopt Polish techniques but they were enlight[ened]..." — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Welchman on the doubled indicator: "The double encipherment of each text setting ... was a gross error. It enabled us to attack the million-odd combinations of wheel order and ring settings without bickering about the vast number of steckerboard cross-connections" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Rejewski's 1979 critique of the British official history: "we quickly found the [wirings] within the [new rotors], but ..." (their problem was resources, not mathematics) — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

**Myths and errors in popular accounts (with the correcting source)**
- "Turing alone broke Enigma": the rotor wirings, the permutation attack, the first catalogue, the first sheets and the first "bomba" were Polish (1932–38); the bombe was engineered by Keen, made effective by Welchman's diagonal board, and fed by cribs from many analysts — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); [Wikipedia, Rejewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_Rejewski)
- "Colossus broke Enigma": Colossus attacked the Lorenz SZ40/42 ("Tunny") teleprinter cipher; bombes, not computers, attacked Enigma — [Wikipedia, Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_(cryptography))
- "Americans captured the Enigma from U-110" (film U-571, 2000): the capture was by HMS Bulldog's boarding party on 9 May 1941 — [Wikipedia, U-110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_submarine_U-110_(1940))
- "A letter can encrypt to itself" / "the reflector was a strength": the reflector makes the cipher self-reciprocal but guarantees no letter maps to itself, the flaw cribs depend on — [Wikipedia, Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine); [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- "The key space (10²³) made Enigma unbreakable": attacks never searched the plugboard; conjugation invariance and cribs sidestepped it (Q2, Q4) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- "Double stepping: ADU → AEV → BFW" (as in the task brief): correct is ADU → ADV → AEW → BFX → BFY — [Wikipedia, Enigma rotor details](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_rotor_details), verified.
- "Bletchley read Enigma continuously from 1940": Shark was dark Feb–Dec 1942 — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- "The Poles gave up in 1939": they handed over working doubles, sheets and the bomba design and kept working at PC Bruno/Cadix — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)

### Inferences
- Story-mode beats with the strongest documentary support: (1) 9 Dec 1932 Schmidt's tables and the ETW guess; (2) the 1937 reflector swap wiping a year's catalogue; (3) 15 Sept 1938 and the two-month Polish sprint to sheets and bombas; (4) Pyry, July 1939 (with Knox's ETW question); (5) 1 May 1940 and the Herivel tip three weeks later; (6) Agnus Dei and the diagonal board; (7) U-110 and Shark; (8) U-559 and the December 1942 re-entry; (9) 1974 disclosure and Rejewski's late recognition.

### Gaps
- Exact Pyry dates (25 vs 26–27 July 1939) and the Shark re-entry date (12 vs 13 December 1942) differ between sources; a primary source (Denniston's account; Hut 8 history) was not fetched.
- The 1936 procedure changes and the count of US Navy bombes are unverified (see Q3, Q5 gaps).

## Key Question 7: Which mathematical results are most illuminating to animate

### Takeaway
Six results carry the whole story and are all small enough to animate faithfully: conjugation preserves cycle structure; products of two fixed-point-free involutions have paired cycles; the 105,456-setting catalogue versus ~10⁶ signatures; the odometer double-step; ring-vs-position shift; and loop-driven contradiction in the bombe (with the diagonal board as reciprocity).

### Cited Findings
- Conjugation invariance and its consequence "the effect of the plugboard has been nullified!" — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf); the constructive proof (write Y's cycles beneath X's) — [Vábek](https://physics.mff.cuni.cz/wds/proc/pdf06/WDS06_122_m1_Vabek.pdf)
- Paired-cycle theorem with explicit construction XY = (a₁a₃…)(a₂ₖ…a₂) — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- Cyclometer as a physical animation of a cycle: current "continues until the charge returns to A"; eight lamps for a pair of 4-cycles — [Christensen](https://www.matematiksider.dk/enigma/MAA%20article%20about%20Enigma%20.pdf)
- 105,456 settings vs "roughly 1 million" characteristics — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma)
- Females: 1 in 8 messages; sheet superposition "the number of visible apertures gradually decreased" — [Wikipedia, Cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis_of_the_Enigma); [Wikipedia, Zygalski sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygalski_sheets)
- Bombe loops: expected stops 40,000 (0 loops, 8 letters) vs 2.2 (3 loops, 8 letters) — [Wikipedia, Bombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombe); the 1/25/26-lamp test — [Tony Sale](https://www.codesandciphers.org.uk/virtualbp/tbombe/tbombe.htm)
- Double stepping and the 16,900 period — [Wikipedia, Enigma machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_machine); ring setting moving wiring but not the turnover letter — [Rijmenants](https://www.ciphermachinesandcryptology.com/en/enigmatech.htm)
- Herivel square clustering from 17,576 to "6 to 30" candidates — [Wikipedia, Herivel tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herivel_tip)

### Inferences (recommended animations, in story order)
1. Rotor anatomy: drag the ring relative to the wiring core; show A→E become A→K at ring B (verified), and the window letter Q still triggering the carry.
2. Odometer with a glitch: step through ADU→ADV→AEW→BFX and highlight the pawl catching the middle rotor's notch twice; contrast with the 26×26×26 a naive viewer expects (16,900 vs 17,576).
3. "No letter to itself": light the lamp board for a fixed position and show the 13 reciprocal pairs; then slide a crib until no crash remains (use ATTACKATDAWN/WSNPNLKLSTCS).
4. Conjugation: apply a random plugboard S to a permutation drawn as cycles; the letters relabel, the cycle shapes do not. Then compute AD from the 65 historical indicators live and show (10 10 2 2 1 1).
5. Paired cycles: build X=(ab)(cd)(ef), Y=(bc)(de)(fa) as a hexagon of alternating edges and watch XY split into two opposite 3-cycles (ace)(bfd); then generalize to 26 letters.
6. Catalogue lookup: 105,456 settings hashed into 101³ ≈ 1.03 million signature buckets; type today's (10 10 2 2 1 1 | 9 9 3 3 1 1 | 13 13) and see the handful of candidates.
7. Zygalski sheets: 26 stacked 51×51 grids on a light table, holes narrowing as sheets are offset by each message's female.
8. Bombe: 12 scramblers wired by the menu; inject a voltage on one Stecker hypothesis; watch it flood 26 wires (reject) or stay on 1/25 (stop); toggle the diagonal board to show how reciprocity closes extra loops and kills false stops.
9. Herivel square: plot first indicators of the day; the cluster reveals the Ringstellung.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gives a ready-made table of the distribution of cycle types across the 105,456 settings (Carter's and Kuhl's reconstructions are cited by Christensen but not online here); the app can compute it directly from the verified core in seconds.
